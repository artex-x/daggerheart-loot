# 2026-10-29 - The orchestrator reads the plan limits and winds work down before a cut

- Amended by "Usage guards park work, never change a tier, and resume AFK work after a reset" (2026-10-29): the weekly 98 % wrap-up, the resume after a reset, the progress line, the usage log, the half-context session and no tier change.
- Task: `usage-guards` (owner, 2026-10-29, chat: the thresholds; the planner: the mechanism).
- Decision: On the desktop app the orchestrator reads `mcp__ccd_session_mgmt__get_usage` before each dispatch and each resume, at each task notification, and at a recurring session cron tick while one of its subagents runs: every 15 minutes below a 5-hour reading of 75 %, every 5 minutes from 75 % until the wrap-up is sent. At 90 % of the 5-hour limit it dispatches and resumes nothing and sends each running subagent one fixed wrap-up message. At 95 % of the weekly all-models limit it dispatches and resumes nothing. A wrapped-up worker commits only a batch whose gates passed, reverts nothing, and leaves its edits and its handoff in the worktree. The rule and the message live in `.claude/prompts/orchestrate.prompt.md`, "Usage guards"; the measured facts in `.claude/README.md`, "Usage guards".
- Rejected: a hook guard (a command hook cannot call an MCP tool, and the one cut on 2026-10-29 happened with no rule in place, so a prose rule comes first); usage checks inside every worker role (each role loads the tool and needs checkpoints that a planner or a reviewer does not have; one orchestrator tick covers every role); a 4-minute tick (the main session's prompt cache lives 1 hour, so a 15-minute gap still reads the cache, and 15 ticks an hour cost about four times the turns and the context growth of 4); a revert of partial edits at the wrap-up (it discards paid work that the worktree keeps through the reset).
- Accepted trade-off: each tick is one orchestrator turn of about two cache reads of its context, ending with one status line, while a subagent runs; a recurring job fires up to half its period late, so 15-minute ticks can be about 22 minutes apart, which the 75 % switch to 5 minutes covers; a subagent inside one long foreground check reads the wrap-up only when that check ends, which costs wall clock, not usage.
- Evidence: the 2026-10-29 cut stopped a task 57 implementer with about 110 uncommitted files; the measurements behind the mechanism are in `.claude/README.md`, "Usage guards".
