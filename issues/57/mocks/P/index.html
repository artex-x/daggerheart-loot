  • QP.2: the standard card is 63.5 x 88.9 mm (2.5 x 3.5 in); nine fit on A4 with 7.75 mm side and 13.15 mm top and bottom margins.
  • QP.3: the compact card stays 44 x 63 mm.
  • QP.4: the rules text starts at 4.0cqw on the standard card (7.1 pt) and at 4.6cqw on the compact card (5.7 pt), with today's ladder below.

Sleeves (checked 2026-10-29, not measured by hand): 63.5 x 88.9 mm is the standard card size that standard sleeves (about 66 x 91 mm) are made for, and today's 63 x 88 fits them too. 44 x 63 mm is 3 mm wider than a Mini American card (41 x 63 mm), so it does not fit Mini American sleeves (about 43-44 x 65 mm); it fits Mini European sleeves (about 45-46 x 68-71 mm, made for 44 x 68 mm cards) with about 5 mm spare in length. Sources: the BoardGameGeek "Card Sleeves" wiki and the Mayday and Gamegenic size listings.

QP.1 - Which face prints the cards?

The owner asked which face Daggerheart cards use. The Core Set PDF's card pages (PDF page 27, printed page 26, the one card figure; PDF page 330, printed page 329, the domain card reference) embed three faces (pdffonts): Questa Sans Light and Questa Sans Bold for the feature text and the bold terms, Questa Sans Light Italic, and Eveleth Clean Regular for the card name and the type banner. Overpass, Questa Slab and Capitana are in other parts of the book, not on these pages.

The paper floor on the compact card (RP-2)

Before round 5 the smallest compact text was 3.9 pt (EN) and 4.2 pt (RU), under DESIGN.md's paper floor of 4.5 pt for labels. Two elements went under it, found in the catalog run (1459 records): (1) the damage label in the strip, on the 70 EN compact cards that carry a modifier beside the label (for example Dread GM Toolbox weapons): the ribbon fit shrank that one label to 2.2cqw (3.9 pt) and ignored the floor; (2) the rules text of three RU colour compact cards with a long text (voa2_a1, voa4_a3, dv14), which the ladder took down to 3.4cqw (4.2-4.4 pt). The fix drawn in the compact rows above: the label shrink stops at 4.5 pt (3.7cqw on the 44 mm card), and the compact ladder's floor rises from 2.6cqw to 3.7cqw (4.55 pt); the standard card's floor stays 2.6cqw (4.7 pt on 63.5 mm). Measured: the smallest text of every compact layout is now 4.5 pt; the smallest rules text is 4.7 pt RU colour, 4.6 pt RU black and white, 4.7 pt EN colour, 4.9 pt EN black and white; no catalog card clips. The cost: 3 more RU colour compact cards lose their picture (20, from 17); the EN counts do not change. Open for batch P: at 4.5 pt the word "DAMAGE" (21.6 px) is 2.6 px wider than its box, so it touches the modifier and the ribbon notch on those 70 EN cards; the strip needs 3 px more room for that label (a narrower modifier badge), or the batch names a shorter label. No Figma node draws this case.

Source Sans 3

The same face as the UI and the share cards, served by the app, so a sheet measures and prints the same on every machine; the closest free match to the cards' humanist text face; narrower than Segoe UI, so fewer cards shrink and fewer names take three lines. Trade-off: the sheet fits again once the face has loaded, and the print button waits for that; an offline tab with no cached face prints in the fallback.

Overpass

Free and Cyrillic, but a grotesque that the cards do not use, and a second face to load beside the UI face. Shown here once its files are on the host.

Today's stack

Keeps every measured figure on this host, but the print differs by machine (Inter, SF, Segoe UI or Roboto), so a sheet can fit on one computer and clip on another.

QP.8 - Cards whose picture gives way

With the larger text, the longest colour texts take the whole card and the picture is hidden: 10 RU and 1 EN standard cards, 20 RU and 8 EN compact cards in Source Sans 3. Today the same happens to 2 RU standard cards and 3 RU compact cards, and those cards keep a blank band of paper at the top, where the art padding was (about a quarter of the card). The Core Set cards always keep about half the card for the art.

The options

Keep the ladder order, drop the blank band (drawn)

The text shrinks only as far as the card needs, and the picture gives way on the few longest cards: the players read the text at the table. A card that loses its picture also loses the blank band and fits its text again from the base in the whole card, so it prints larger text than today. The renders on this page use this rule.

An art floor

The picture keeps at least a quarter of the card; those cards then print their text smaller (towards the 2.6cqw floor, about 4.6 pt).

  • The rules text starts at 4.0cqw on the standard card and 4.6cqw on the compact card, not 3.5cqw; the compact card is no longer the standard card at 70% (QP.4, answered).

Against the Core Set cards (style only, nothing copied)

Reference: PDF page 27 (printed page 26) and PDF page 330 (printed page 329). The reference card prints its feature text at about 7 pt, gives the art about 49% of the card and centres the name over the text. The proposal brings the standard text to 7.1 pt (from 6.2) and the compact text to 5.7 pt (from 4.3), so the text block reads at the reference's density; the art keeps a median of about two thirds of the card (more than the reference), and gives way only on the longest texts (QP.8). The hierarchy stays the node's: tags, then a heavy capital name, then the stat ribbon, then the text, on one left axis (QP.5).

Impeccable critique, with the Clarity Rule

  • Hierarchy: the name, the stat ribbon and the text read in that order on every kind; the larger text does not compete with the name (10.2 pt against 7.1 pt).
  • Clarity: the print bar loses the A4 sentence and the note under the switches; the size switch says the size («63×88»), so no control needs a sentence.
  • Consistency: the fixed bottom bar at 360 is the item sheet's pattern; the switches stay where the choice is made (Q3.2).
  • Open: the light no-picture well (mock 1b) and the neutral pressed switches (batch 1) are not in these renders.
